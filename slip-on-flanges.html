Slip-on flanges, sometimes referred to as ‘hubbed flanges’, have a low profile hub. This flange type is usually connected to a pipe via one or two crevice welds (one outside and one inside the flange), although a single weld is also attainable.

These flanges are available in various sizes and are commonly favored for ASME class ≤ 600 low-pressure applications. The bore size of a slip-on flange (internal diameter) is larger than its connecting pipe, thus allowing it to slip over the pipe.

However, due to the absent full penetration weld between the pipe and flange, its strength is significantly low and limits its application. In some cases, If a welding neck flange cannot be used because of space constraints, this Slip-on flange can be a plausible substitute.

Features of Slip-On-Flange

Slip-on flanges are renowned for their low cost of materials and convenient setup. An extra benefit of these flanges is that they can also be utilized as lap-joint flanges if either Type B or Type C ends are being managed.

Slip-on-flanges offer a vast selection of sizes as well as low-stress designs to settle on. Unlike welding neck flanges, there’s no neck to attach to the pipeline, so double welding is essential.

Slip-on flanges are ideal for reduced-pressure situations and are quite simple to assemble and weld to differing tubes.

Slip-on flanges do not necessitate a significant longitudinal gap in the pipeline for installation.

Benefits of Slip-On Flanges

  • Slip-On-Flanges have Low-cost installation
  • Less time is needed to be spent on ensuring the accuracy of the cut pipe
  • They are somewhat easier to align
  • The slip-on flanges have low hubs because the pipe slips into the flange before welding
  • Slip-on-flanges are welded from the inside as well as the outside to ensure their strength
  • They prevent leakage
